**Անունովդ ապրիս**

_(Anunovd apris / Anounovt abris)_

This is the traditional Armenian name-day blessing. It means **“May you live with your name”** or **“May you live up to your name.”**

In Armenian culture a person’s name carries spiritual weight. Many names are tied to saints, virtues, or the Holy Mother of God. The blessing expresses the hope that we will live a long and worthy life that honors the meaning and example of our name — and of the saint associated with it.

It is the classic greeting given on someone’s name day (_anvan or_ / _anoonin or_), the feast day of the saint after whom they are named. It is often said instead of (or together with) “Happy Name Day” / Շնորհավոր անվան օրը.

For **St. Mary**, this Sunday is our parish’s name day. Saying **Անունովդ ապրիս** is therefore a fitting traditional blessing for our church and for all who bear names related to the Holy Mother of God.

Մարիամ and its short form Մարո are the most direct. The longer list reflects the Armenian Church’s traditional practice of grouping many feminine names ending in _\-uhi_ (“lady”) and other Marian-associated names under the Assumption feast.

## Join Us for the Feast, Blessing of the Grapes & Madagh

On Sunday, August 16, 2026, **St. Mary** will observe the Feast of the Assumption of the Holy Mother of God — one of the five major Feast Days of the Armenian Church.

As Holy Scripture calls us to honor the Lord with the firstfruits of our labor, our Church blesses the grapes after Divine Liturgy. The grape holds a place of honor among all fruits, for from it comes the wine used in the Sacraments of the Divine Liturgy, Holy Communion, and Holy Matrimony.

**Madagh** is an act of mercy, thanksgiving, and almsgiving. On major feasts and our Church name day, the faithful offer Madagh in gratitude to God for health and well-being, and to beseech spiritual peace from the Almighty.
